- **The discrepancy this experiment exists to explain.** I measured the adversarial-rename accuracy cost on Gemma-3-4B-it at **+0.0117 [−0.037, +0.061]** over 257 HumanEval-X Python/JS snippets ([`2026-09-14_bigger-corpus-results.md`](2026-09-14_bigger-corpus-results.md)). They measure −0.363 on HumanEval-X Java. **Both cannot be measuring the same thing.** Five differences, ordered by my estimate of importance:
  1. **Task format.** Theirs is *verification*: 8–16 boolean case expressions per snippet, answered as one JSON object of `case_id → "T"/"F"`. Mine is *output prediction* graded by exact match. Chance is **50 %** for them and ~0 % for me — and their renamed score of **40.20 is below chance**, which is not a comprehension deficit but a systematic inversion.
  2. **The method name is inside the question.** Every case reads `s.hasCloseElements(new ArrayList<>(...), 0.3)`. Renaming the method rewrites the *prompt's question text* 14 times over, not merely the code. A model reading intent off the call name loses that cue entirely; that is a much stronger manipulation than renaming body identifiers.
  3. **Negation pairs.** Cases are generated as `expr` / `!(expr)` (`mutation_type: negation_fallback`), so a consistent evaluator scores both or neither and the effective n is ~half the case count.
  4. **Java**, not Python/JS; and **Qwen2.5/DeepSeek coder models**, both barred here.
  5. Their renaming scheme is undescribed beyond "disrupts lexical cues"; ours is *adversarially misleading*, which should be at least as damaging.
