- **Statistical note (a deviation from the prereg's letter, recorded because it changes the method text but not the test).** The prereg specified LOO-CV logistic regression per feature. A 1-D logistic is **monotone** in its feature, so its LOO score is an affine function of `x` and its LOO AUC is either `AUC(y,x)` or `1 − AUC(y,x)`: the cross-validation was doing exactly one thing, choosing the sign without peeking at the labels. A **two-sided** statistic `|AUC − 0.5|` makes the sign free, so it is the identical hypothesis test with no fitting — and it runs the full 10 000 permutations instead of the 100 the fitted version could afford in the time budget. Reported `auc` is `max(a, 1−a)` with `direction` recorded; the null statistic is two-sided as well, so selecting the sign inflates nothing. The first attempt (fitted, 100 permutations) was killed on wall-clock and **no numbers from it are quoted**.

- **What worked / hypothesis verdict:**
  - **H-A10a → `SELECTOR-CHEAP-ABSENT`, prediction ✓ SUPPORTED** (my third hit of seven today). No feature clears the bar; the best is `harmed : l1b_prompt_len` at AUC 0.645, and **every observed AUC lies at or below its own permutation p95**. Nothing here is even nominally interesting, and the smallest q is 0.805.
  - **The null distributions are the entry's real finding.** With 7 (or 6) positives in 60 items, the two-sided max-AUC statistic has a **null mean of 0.55–0.60 and a p95 of 0.62–0.74**. So on this corpus **any single-feature AUC below ≈ 0.74 is indistinguishable from chance**, which is a hard, quantified statement about what the 60-item set can and cannot support — not a vague power caveat. A naive reading of "AUC 0.645" as weak evidence would have been wrong by construction, and this is exactly the comparison that makes it visible.
  - `noop_parsed_rate`, my nominated candidate, is the *worst* feature on `harmed` (AUC 0.506, p = 0.947). H-A8's finding that non-answering dominates the failure modes does **not** transfer into a per-item predictor of where the write helps.
- **Observations:**
  - **The deployability rule did most of the work, and it is worth stating as a standing constraint.** Of everything banked for these 60 items, almost nothing is admissible: `dG_S_{swap,c3,edit,foreign,rt}` all score the logp of the **L0 reply** (needs the clean run), `cos_delta_E` and `‖Δ_s‖` are built from `h0`, and `noop_rate`/`*_greedy_correct` need the truth. The admissible set collapses to identifier/span counts, AV editability, prompt length and parse events — all *surface* properties of the obfuscation. **That they fail is a coherent negative: whether the decoy damages comprehension is not readable off how much decoy there is.** Which is consistent with H-W16 (meaning ≈ 51 % / removal ≈ 49 %) and with Paper 2's finding that the HCI collapse needs *semantic displacement ∩ identifier spikes* to co-occur, not merely many renamed identifiers.
  - **What this licenses, and what it does not.** It licenses H-A10b: the selector candidates that remain are *behavioural* (self-consistency / answer entropy on the obfuscated prompt for **this** host) rather than structural. It does **not** license "no selector exists" — with a null p95 of 0.74, this corpus could not have detected a genuinely useful AUC of 0.70 even if one were there. **The binding constraint on the whole accuracy arm is n = 60 with 7 flippable, which is H-S14, not a feature-engineering problem.**
