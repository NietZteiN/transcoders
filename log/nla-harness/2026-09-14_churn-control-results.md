- **What worked / hypothesis verdict:**
  - **H-A13 ✗ REFUTED as predicted-`CHURN-DOMINATES`; resolved `CHURN-SUBDOMINANT`.** My reasoning was that the reverse cell could not be a real comprehension effect, therefore it had to be measurement noise, therefore most of the flippable cell was too. The middle step was wrong: "implausible as a comprehension effect" does not imply "an artifact of decoding". The labels are stable — 90 % of flippable items reproduce — so **the discordant cells are reproducible per-item behaviour of the model, not churn.** Fourth prediction missed today where I substituted plausibility for measurement, and the third time a control I built to check myself is what caught it.
  - **The decisive quantitative separation: 37 % token divergence against 3 % label divergence.** Decoding non-determinism is pervasive (a third of generations differ somewhere) but almost never reaches the answer. Two consequences beyond this hypothesis. (a) Every **teacher-forced** readout in this programme (`G_sum`, every `dG_*`) scores a *banked* reply and so is untouched by this entirely. (b) Every **generative** accuracy measurement carries a ~3 % per-item label floor, which is small against the arms that matter (`edit_single` −0.121) but comparable to the ones that do not (`prompt` +0.010, `edit_band` +0.008) — those two were never distinguishable from this floor and should not be quoted as point estimates.

That is right for the **population** claim and wrong for the **per-item** one: a population net of zero means as many items are rename-*helped* as rename-*hurt*, not that no item is hurt. With 90 % label reproducibility those two readings are separable, which is exactly what this control licenses.
