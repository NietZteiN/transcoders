- **What worked / hypothesis verdict:**
  - **H-S14a: my 25–35 prediction is ✗ REFUTED at 22** — outside the interval, though only just. The prediction that *mattered* is refuted much harder: **the enlarged flippable set does not deliver the power it was built for.** 22 flippable against 19 reverse is a net of **3 items**, and the rescue test the whole run was justified by cannot be run on a set whose net content is 3 items.

  - **What the corpus bought, stated plainly:** not power for a rescue test, but a 4.3×-tighter bound on the effect being rescued — and that bound is ~1 %. That is a real deliverable and it kills the rescue programme rather than enabling it.
- **Observations:**
  - **The rename reshuffles correctness without reducing it.** 22 items flip one way, 19 the other, net 3. The partial churn control (job 394681, 64 items twice) shows this is **largely reproducible, not decoding noise**: token-level divergence is 34–36 % but *label* flips are only 1.6 % (L0) / 3.1 % (L1b), and 4 of 5 flippable items stay flippable on re-run. So the discordant cells are real per-item behaviour.
  - **That is structurally the same finding as H-A8c.** Steering gave 6 fixed / 6 broke, pooled exactly 0.0000; the obfuscation itself gives 22 / 19, pooled +0.0117. **Obfuscation and intervention both relocate correctness rather than changing its total** — two independent measurements, one shape. This is the most interesting thing in the accuracy arm and it was invisible at n = 60.
  - **Correction to my own reasoning, twice in one night.** I first read the symmetric discordant cells as proof the flippable set was mostly decoding noise ("~11 of 15 are churn"); the churn control refuted that (labels are stable; it is *behaviour*). I then quoted a required corpus size of ~1 500 items and revised to ~14 800 an hour later as the ratio drifted; the final figure on the complete data is **9 140**. Both errors came from inferring from plausibility instead of measuring, and both were caught by controls rather than by re-reading.
