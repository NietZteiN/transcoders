### Target Date: 2026-09-14 (AMENDMENT to H-R2 — three arms added at the user's request, and the alignment dependency they share)

- **Why.** The user asked what other NLA-style steering methods are worth testing. Three are added to the H-R2 bake-off ([`2026-09-14_steering-bakeoff-prereg.md`](2026-09-14_steering-bakeoff-prereg.md)) **before any steering data exists**, so no threshold is being chosen against a result. Two of the three are controls that the original 8-arm list was weaker for lacking, which is why they go in rather than being held.
  - **`renamed_swap_oracle`** — write the ORIGINAL code's residual at the renamed identifier positions. This is the **ceiling for any span-position residual intervention**: if the oracle cannot recover the damage, neither `erasure`, nor a trained NLA pair, nor any other write at those positions can. It bounds three arms at once for ~0.5 GPU-h, and on our own corpus the equivalent arm (`c3`/`swap`) is what revealed the reconstruction headroom was unreachable at span positions.
  - **`renamed_foreign`** — write a DIFFERENT snippet's clean state at the same positions. The specificity control: without it, any gain from `swap_oracle` or `erasure` could be perturbation rather than content. This is the same role `foreign` plays throughout the W and E families.
  - **`renamed_combined`** — CodeSteer **and** our erasure vector together. The only genuinely novel arm: attention reallocation and residual editing are different channels and nobody has tested whether they compose. Pre-declared reading: **super-additive** ⇒ the channels carry different information; **sub-additive or equal to the better single arm** ⇒ evidence they are the same operation by different means, which is this programme's central claim tested directly rather than argued.
- **Held, not dropped:** `erasure_band` (β = 0.35 over L2–13) versus single-layer full replacement — our β-sweep preferred the band and today's accuracy work found full replacement actively harms (−0.121 [−0.206, −0.035]); and **answer-position writes**, still untried, which H-A8 makes interesting because non-answering dominates the failure modes. Both are one arm each and can be added once the gate reports.
- **The shared dependency, stated because it is the real cost.** `swap_oracle`, `foreign` and `erasure` all write at token positions corresponding to the renamed identifiers, and they need the **original** residual at the **renamed** positions. Decoy names tokenise to different lengths, so the two sequences do not align positionally — this is exactly the problem `repair_pairs.recover` solved for Python/JS (precision/recall 1.000 after two zero-position bugs produced three artifact verdicts in one day, defects #8/#9). **It does not exist for Java and must be built and gated before those three arms can run.** The rename manifest gives the correspondence (original name → decoy name per snippet), so the mapping is computable; the gate is that every span must resolve in both sequences with the same count, and any snippet that fails is excluded rather than silently mis-written.
- **Unchanged:** every H-R2a/b/c threshold, the subset rule (first 50 by id, `PACKS-PAIRED`), the decoding regime, and the gating of the whole bake-off on H-R1b finding damage to recover. Arm count 8 → 11, cost ≈ 5 → ≈ 7 GPU-h.
- **Also recorded:** the trained NLA pair still cannot enter this bake-off — no Gemma backend exists in their `steering/backends/` (llama, qwen2, qwen3, deepseek only), and our AV/AR are Gemma-3-4B/12B trained on Python/JS residuals against a Java corpus. Writing a Gemma-3 backend (~150 lines mirroring `llama_backend.py`) would let every arm including the trained pair run on one host, but the cross-language transfer would be untested and a null uninterpretable. Deferred as a separate decision after the gate; training a Java pair (~30+ GPU-h) is the clean version and is not justified until damage is shown to exist.
