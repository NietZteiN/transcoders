- **What worked / hypothesis verdict:**
  - **H-R1a ✓ SUPPORTED, and strongly.** Original P@1 0.7488 (Llama) and 0.7995 (both coder models) all sit in the band, and the case-pack totals reproduce their published counts to 0.4 %. **The harness is not the explanation for anything below.**
  - **H-R1b ✗ `DAMAGE-ABSENT` on Llama-3.1-8B: +0.0300 [−0.0553, +0.1119] against their −0.363.** This is the entry's central result. My earlier Python/JS output-prediction measurement gave **+0.0117** ([`2026-09-14_bigger-corpus-results.md`](2026-09-14_bigger-corpus-results.md)); under *their* protocol, *their* language, *their* case construction and *their* metric it gives **+0.0300**. **The two agree, so my null was not a protocol artifact** — which is precisely what this replication was built to test, and it failed to overturn it.
  - **H-R1c → `DAMAGE-PARTIAL`, prediction ✓ SUPPORTED (my 4th hit of 11 today, and the first prediction about a mechanism I got right).** The drop scales with code specialisation: **+0.030** general → **+0.076** / **+0.113** coder, i.e. 2.5–4× larger on coder models at the same scale, same corpus, same packs, same renamer. So name-reliance does look like a property of code-specialised models, matching Papers 2–3's split (coder ρ ≈ 0 vs reasoning-tuned 0.30–0.47) and their own Table 4, where the identical transform costs **−36.3 on HumanEval-X but only −8.6 on CruxEval-X**. An effect that swings 4× across datasets within their own paper was already fragile.
  - **StarCoder2-15B excluded by the pre-registered rule, not by judgement.** `parsed_frac` **0.1325** with `parse_mode: None` on all 150 runs — it emits 700–1 400 characters and almost never the required single JSON object, so unparsed cases score wrong and it lands *below* chance at 0.099. The `PROTOCOL-BROKEN` threshold was written to catch exactly this and it did. **This is a limitation of their protocol worth recording: the T/F-JSON format demands strong instruction-following, so a model can score below chance purely by ignoring the output format**, and any model comparison under it is restricted to models that comply.
- **Observations:**
  - **The gap that remains is 3× and has two candidate causes, only one of which I can rule on.** Even the most affected model (CodeLlama-7B, +0.113) shows a third of their drop. (a) **Model family** — their four models are Qwen2.5-7B/14B and DeepSeek-6.7B/V2-Lite, all barred here, so Qwen-specific name-reliance cannot be tested in this project at all. (b) **Renamer strength** — mine is mine, because theirs is excluded from the artifact (`GITREADY_MANIFEST.md`), and it renames a mean of **3.4 identifiers per snippet**. It does rename the method, which puts the decoy inside every case expression, and it is execution-validated; but if theirs also renames fields, types and every local, it is a stronger manipulation. **This was declared as an unresolvable confound before the run and it remains one.**
  - **The bake-off gating decision.** H-R1b's `DAMAGE-ABSENT` on Llama would have voided the bake-off under the frozen rule; H-R1c's `DAMAGE-PARTIAL` on CodeLlama-7B (0.7995 → 0.6866, **11.3 points of headroom**) licenses it on that model instead. Running the arms on Llama would have been measuring recovery of a 3-point non-effect.
  - Two things cost time and are recorded so they are not repeated: the public paper named *CodeSteer* (arXiv 2502.04350) is an **unrelated** symbolic-reasoning method, and I searched for it before the artifact arrived; and job 394532 used `host_traces`' default HF multimodal loader at 4.8 min/item (20 h projected) before `--fast-host`, while 394561 died on an argparse flag my own patch had failed to write.
